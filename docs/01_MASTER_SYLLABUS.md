# AI-POWERED FINANCE, INVESTING, TRADING & QUANTITATIVE FINANCE

## Final Master Syllabus — Version 2.0

---

# 1. PROGRAM PURPOSE

This curriculum is designed to take a learner with limited financial-market knowledge from basic financial literacy to the ability to independently research investments, construct portfolios, evaluate systematic strategies, use quantitative methods, and build AI-assisted investment systems.

The learner should ultimately be able to combine:

**financial judgment + business understanding + market knowledge + quantitative reasoning + data + technology + AI + disciplined risk management**

into a repeatable investment process.

The program is not designed around predicting the next stock move, maximising trading frequency, or blindly automating investment decisions.

Its objective is to improve:

- financial decision quality;
- understanding of markets;
- investment analysis;
- portfolio construction;
- risk management;
- quantitative reasoning;
- research productivity;
- technological leverage;
- ability to detect weak evidence and false confidence.

Investment returns are an outcome.  
**Decision quality and process quality are the primary learning objectives.**

---

# 2. PROGRAM STRUCTURE

The curriculum contains six stages.

## STAGE I — PERSONAL FINANCE & MARKET FOUNDATIONS

Levels 0–2

Understand money, financial planning, markets, asset classes and investment products.

## STAGE II — INVESTMENT ANALYSIS

Levels 3–7

Understand businesses, accounting, valuation, macroeconomics, bonds and credit.

## STAGE III — PORTFOLIOS, RISK & MARKET MECHANICS

Levels 8–11

Learn portfolio construction, behavioural finance, trading mechanics and derivatives.

## STAGE IV — QUANTITATIVE FINANCE & STRATEGY RESEARCH

Levels 12–17

Learn mathematics, statistics, programming, time series, backtesting, systematic investing and machine learning.

## STAGE V — AI, DATA & AUTOMATION

Levels 18–22

Learn LLMs, agents, financial data infrastructure, APIs and controlled automation.

## STAGE VI — PROFESSIONAL INVESTMENT PRACTICE

Levels 23–25 + Final Capstone

Integrate regulation, historical lessons, professional research processes and the learner's Personal Investment Operating System.

# MASTER TEACHING PRINCIPLES

These principles apply to every module generated from this syllabus.

## Intuition Before Formalism

Every difficult idea should first be explained intuitively.

## Why Before How

The learner should understand why a concept exists before learning formulas or procedures.

## Mathematics With Meaning

No formula should appear without:

explaining every variable;

showing intuition;

working through a realistic example;

explaining limitations.

## Progressive Complexity

Each concept should progress:

intuition  
→ simple example  
→ formal explanation  
→ realistic example  
→ edge cases  
→ application.

## Evidence Before Narrative

Claims should distinguish:

fact;

assumption;

inference;

hypothesis;

opinion.

## Current Facts Must Be Verified

Current:

taxes;

regulations;

market rules;

interest rates;

financial data;

company information

must be checked against authoritative sources.

## India + Global Context

Indian examples should be heavily represented while preserving global relevance.

## Quantitative Discipline

All strategy evaluation should consider:

transaction costs;

slippage;

liquidity;

turnover;

survivorship bias;

look-ahead bias;

data leakage;

overfitting;

out-of-sample performance.

## Risk Before Return

Every investment or strategy must examine downside before expected return.

## Leverage Requires Extra Scrutiny

Leverage must never be presented without analysing loss amplification and risk of ruin.

## Derivatives Are Risk Instruments First

Derivatives should first be understood as mechanisms for transferring and restructuring risk.

## AI Is an Assistant, Not an Oracle

AI-generated analysis should be independently verifiable.

## Complexity Must Earn Its Place

A sophisticated model must outperform simpler alternatives on credible evidence, not merely produce a more impressive explanation.

## Reproducibility Matters

Important research should be capable of being reproduced later from documented:

data;

code;

parameters;

assumptions;

versions.

## Decision Quality Is Separate From Outcome

Good decisions can lose money.

Bad decisions can make money.

Investment skill must therefore be evaluated through process as well as outcomes.
