1.1

Why Markets Exist

Markets connect people with savings to people and organisations that can use capital, then make those claims transferable and priced.

The gap markets close

Households often earn before they need to spend. Businesses and governments often need money before their projects produce cash. A financial market lets one side provide capital in exchange for a claim on future cash flows or ownership.

Savers

Delay consumption and supply funds.

↔

Capital users

Issue ownership or borrowing claims and put funds to work.

Seven jobs, not one

Capital formation turns dispersed savings into usable pools of funding. Borrowing moves purchasing power across time. Investment allocates saved money to productive or income-producing claims. Price discovery aggregates competing orders into transaction prices. [[liquidity|Liquidity]] allows an owner to exit without waiting for the issuer to repay. [[risk-transfer|Risk transfer]] moves exposure to someone more willing or able to bear it.

The seventh job is information. Prices, volumes and yields compress many beliefs into observable signals. They are useful signals, not verdicts. A price can be wrong, manipulated, stale or driven by forced trading.

Three kinds of promise

Equity

A residual ownership claim. Payment is not fixed; holders receive what remains after prior claims.

Debt

A contractual claim for interest and repayment, subject to default risk.

Derivative

A contract whose value depends on another asset, rate or event. Full treatment comes in Level 11.

Cash

The settlement asset used to complete obligations, not a promise of investment return.

Common misconception

“The stock market funds companies every time shares trade.”

Why this failsMost exchange volume is secondary trading among investors. Its contribution is indirect: liquidity and observable pricing can make original issuance easier.
