Market Capitalisation

Market capitalisation is the market price of one share multiplied by all shares outstanding. It measures equity value, not business size in every sense.

From one share to the whole equity claim

{{i:function}} Formula

Market capitalisation=Share price×Shares outstanding
Share price
Current market price per share, in ₹.
Shares outstanding
Total issued equity shares currently outstanding.

Worked example. A hypothetical company has 10 crore shares at ₹240. Its market capitalisation is ₹2,400 crore.

Limitation. It excludes debt and cash. Enterprise value arrives in Level 5.

Full value and investable float

Free float excludes strategic holdings that are not normally available for public trading, such as many promoter holdings. Free-float market capitalisation multiplies full market capitalisation by the investible-weight factor.

{{i:calculator}} Worked example

{{tag:hyp}}

At ₹240 with 10 crore shares, full market cap is ₹2,400 crore. If free float is 35%, free-float market cap is ₹840 crore. The company has not become smaller; the measure asks how much equity is reasonably investable.

Free float can influence index weight and tradability, but it is not identical to liquidity. A company can have broad public ownership and still trade poorly at a particular moment.

Large, mid and small are ranking labels

Size bands depend on a methodology, market and review date. A company near a boundary can move categories without a dramatic change in business quality. Treat the label as a portfolio-classification rule, not as a permanent identity or a direct measure of risk.

{{i:prohibit}} Common misconception

“A ₹2,400 crore company has ₹2,400 crore available in cash.”

Why this failsMarket cap is the market value of the equity claim. It is not cash on the balance sheet, annual revenue or the amount every shareholder could realise at the current price.

Better questionWhich measure fits the question: equity value, enterprise value, revenue, assets or available cash?
