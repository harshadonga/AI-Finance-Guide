Part II · Institutions and people

1.5

Market Participants

The same trade can serve different constraints: saving, hedging, rebalancing, market making or a change in belief.

Classify by mandate, not by stereotype

Retail investors

Individuals acting for themselves. Horizons, skill and constraints vary widely.

Asset managers and mutual funds

Invest pooled money under a stated mandate, benchmark and liquidity promise.

Pension funds and insurers

Invest against long-dated obligations, regulation and capital constraints.

Banks

Fund, lend, manage liquidity and make markets within balance-sheet limits.

Foreign portfolio investors

Allocate across countries while bearing currency, regulation and access constraints.

Hedge funds and proprietary traders

Take positions under strategy, funding and risk limits; their time horizons can be short or long.

Market makers

Quote buy and sell prices, earning spread while managing inventory risk.

Promoters and insiders

Hold strategic stakes and face disclosure and dealing restrictions.

A forced trade can move price without changing belief

An index fund may buy because a stock enters its benchmark. A pension fund may sell to meet a policy limit. A market maker may trade to reduce inventory. A foreign investor may sell because of a currency mandate. Order flow therefore contains information and constraints at the same time.

? A company enters a widely tracked index. Some funds must buy it even though their analysts have not changed the valuation. What is the best explanation? - Every fund now believes the company is undervalued || Index funds follow a rule; their trade need not express a valuation view. + Benchmark-linked demand can create a forced flow || The mandate, not a fresh belief, causes the trade. - The exchange instructs all investors to buy || An index change affects mandates, not every market participant. >

Flow and belief are not identical. Later lessons use this distinction when interpreting volume and price moves.

Ask four questions about any participant

  1. Whose money? The participant's, clients', policyholders' or beneficiaries'?
  2. What mandate? Absolute return, benchmark tracking, liability matching, liquidity provision or hedging?
  3. What constraint? Regulation, leverage, redemption, capital, concentration or time?
  4. What would force action? Flows, margin, benchmark change, risk limit or maturity?
