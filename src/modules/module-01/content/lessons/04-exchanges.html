1.4

Exchanges

An exchange is a rulebook, admission system, matching venue, surveillance function and data source, not a warehouse of shares.

What an exchange does

AdmitsSets listing and membership requirements
MatchesRuns trading sessions and priority rules
PublishesDisseminates quotes, trades and reference data
MonitorsSurveils activity and enforces venue rules

NSE and BSE are major Indian exchanges. NYSE and Nasdaq are major US venues. “Nasdaq” can also refer to indices, so always ask whether the writer means the exchange, the Nasdaq Composite or the Nasdaq-100.

Exchange, regulator and infrastructure are different

SEBI

Regulates India's securities market and registered intermediaries.

Exchange

Operates a venue under its rules and regulatory obligations.

Clearing corporation

Calculates and manages settlement obligations.

Depository

Maintains electronic ownership records through participants.

What varies across exchanges

Listings, trading hours, auction design, order types, tick sizes, settlement links and disclosure standards can differ. “Listed on an exchange” does not mean every security trades frequently, has tight spreads or carries the same investor protections in every jurisdiction.

{{i:prohibit}} Common misconception

“The NSE and the Nifty are the same thing.”

Why this failsNSE is an exchange. Nifty is a family of indices managed by NSE Indices. One is a venue; the other is a measurement rule applied to selected securities.

Better questionAm I discussing where a security trades or how a group of securities is measured?
