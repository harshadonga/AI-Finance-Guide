How Financial Markets Work

Follow money, orders and ownership from a company raising capital to an investor receiving shares.

FoundationalPrerequisite: Module 013 lessons
Big ideaModule mapFinish line

A market is a price system resting on a trust system.

The price system matches competing orders. The trust system makes sure money and securities arrive after strangers trade. Mixing the two produces common errors: treating the last price as fair value, or treating a tap on Buy as completed ownership.

RaiseIssuers exchange claims for new capital in the primary market.
TradeExisting holders and buyers negotiate prices in the secondary market.
SettleClearing and depository systems transfer funds and ownership records.

What you should be able to do

  • Trace a stock purchase from order submission to demat credit.
  • Say who receives the money in a primary issue and in a secondary trade.
  • Choose an order type and state what it cannot guarantee.
  • Read a bid, ask and visible order book, then calculate an average fill.
  • Calculate full and free-float market capitalisation.
  • Explain how index methodology changes weights and interpretation.
  • Reconcile holdings and price series after a corporate action.
  • Separate price return from total return and raw prices from adjusted prices.

Questions worth carrying

Who receives my money?

The answer depends on whether new securities are being issued or an existing holder is selling.

What does a price mean?

It is the latest agreement under a particular market mechanism, not a certificate of value.

What can an order guarantee?

Usually price or urgency, not both. Thin depth makes the trade-off visible.

When do I own the shares?

A match creates a trade. Clearing and settlement complete the transfer.
