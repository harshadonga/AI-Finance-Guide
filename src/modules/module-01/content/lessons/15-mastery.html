Concepts and mechanisms

? Which sequence is correct after a client submits an order that eventually settles? - Broker, depository, issuer, exchange, clearing || The issuer and depository do not match the order. + Broker, exchange match, clearing obligations, settlement, demat record || This separates trading from post-trade completion. - Exchange, issuer, broker, index provider || An index provider is not part of settlement. ? Which transaction normally sends new cash to a company? - One investor buying listed shares from another || That transfers an existing claim. + A fresh issue of shares || The company creates shares and receives the issue proceeds. - An index fund rebalancing || This is secondary-market trading. ? A stock has a last price of ₹250, bid ₹248 and ask ₹254. Which statement is sound? - ₹250 is the price at which any quantity can be sold || The available bid is ₹248 and depth is unknown. + The last trade, current buying offer and current selling offer are different observations || Each field records a different market state. - The fair value is exactly the midpoint, ₹251 || Midpoint is a quote reference, not certified value.

Calculations

? An ask book shows 40 shares at ₹100 and 80 at ₹101. What is the average fill for a 80-share market buy? = The order takes 40 at ₹100 and 40 at ₹101. Average = ₹8,060 ÷ 80 = ₹100.75. ? A company has 6 crore shares, a price of ₹300 and 40% free float. Calculate free-float market cap. = Full market cap = 6 crore × ₹300 = ₹1,800 crore. Free-float market cap = 40% × ₹1,800 crore = ₹720 crore. ? Three old shares at ₹110 give the right to buy one new share at ₹100. Ignoring market movement, calculate the theoretical ex-rights price. = (3 × ₹110 + ₹100) ÷ 4 = ₹107.50.

Application and critique

? You must not pay more than ₹500, but the current ask is ₹507. Which order expresses the constraint? - Market buy || It can execute above ₹500. + Limit buy at ₹500 || It protects the maximum price and may remain unfilled. - Sell stop at ₹500 || Wrong direction and purpose. ? A data vendor shows a 75% overnight price fall on a 4-for-1 split date. What is the first defensible action? - Treat it as a crash and sell || The series may be unadjusted. + Reconcile the corporate action and adjustment method || A 4-for-1 split mechanically quarters the price. - Replace all prices with the index return || That destroys the security's data rather than fixing it. ? A free-float-weighted index rises because its largest constituent rises, while most members fall. Which conclusion is valid? - Most of the market rose || Breadth says the opposite. + The index rose, but constituent breadth was weak || Weighted return and breadth answer different questions. - The index calculation is wrong || Concentration can produce this valid outcome. ? An app claims: “Registered on an app store, therefore SEBI approved.” What is the strongest response? - App stores verify all financial regulation || They do not replace regulatory verification. + Verify the legal entity and registration on SEBI and the relevant exchange || This checks the actual intermediary, not the storefront. - Trust it if brokerage is low || Price does not establish authorisation.
