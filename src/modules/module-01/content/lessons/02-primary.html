Primary Markets

A primary-market transaction creates and allocates a new financial claim; the issuer receives the funding.

Follow the money, not the headline

An offer can contain a fresh issue, where money enters the company, and an offer for sale, where existing owners sell their shares. Both can appear in the same IPO. The total offer size therefore does not automatically equal new capital for the business.

{{i:calculator}} Worked example

{{tag:hyp}}

Nilgiri Provisions offers ₹600 crore of shares. ₹350 crore is a fresh issue and ₹250 crore is an offer for sale. Before costs, the company receives ₹350 crore. Selling shareholders receive ₹250 crore. The company issues more shares for the fresh component, so existing ownership percentages may fall.

A simplified public-issue sequence

PrepareOffer document, risks and use of proceeds
PricePrice band and investor bids
AllocateApplications are accepted under the offer rules
ListAllocated shares become eligible for exchange trading

A listing gain is not evidence that the issue was good, and a weak first day is not proof that the business is poor. The issue price and the first traded price are formed by different processes.

What can go wrong

  • Use-of-proceeds ambiguity: debt repayment, working capital and expansion have different implications.
  • Dilution: more shares can reduce each old share's percentage claim unless value rises enough.
  • Selection pressure: strong demand can produce small allocations; weak demand can be informative but not decisive.
  • Grey-market stories: unofficial indications are not audited evidence and can change abruptly.
