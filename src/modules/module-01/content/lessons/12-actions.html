Corporate Actions

Corporate actions can change units, distribute cash, issue claims or reorganise ownership. The arithmetic must be separated from the economic decision.

Classify the action first

Distribution

Dividends and buybacks move value from the company to participating holders.

Unit change

Splits and bonus issues change shares and reference prices without creating value mechanically.

New claim

Rights issues let eligible holders buy newly issued shares under stated terms.

Reorganisation

Mergers and spin-offs replace or divide claims according to transaction terms.

Rights issues require a decision

Suppose four old shares trade at ₹120 and give the right to buy one new share at ₹80. The theoretical ex-rights price is (4 × 120 + 1 × 80) ÷ 5 = ₹112, ignoring market movement and costs. The right has economic value because choosing not to subscribe while letting the right expire can accept dilution without compensation.

Dates are operational facts

Announcement, ex-date, record date, payment or allotment date and effective date do different jobs. Do not infer entitlement from a headline. Check the exchange notice, transaction terms and settlement rule applicable to the event.

{{i:warning}} Tax boundary

Tax treatment of dividends, buybacks and reorganisations changes over time and belongs to the living regulation module. This lesson covers mechanics only.
