1.3

Secondary Markets

The secondary market transfers existing claims and discovers prices through a defined matching process.

Three ways a trade can form

Continuous order book

Buy and sell orders compete throughout the session. Compatible orders match using the venue's priority rules.

Call auction

Orders collect for a period, then one clearing price is chosen to match as much quantity as possible.

Dealer quotation

Dealers quote prices at which they are prepared to buy or sell. This is common in markets where central books are less dominant.

Negotiated trade

Large or specialised transactions may be agreed under separate venue rules and reported to the market.

Price is an event

The displayed last price records the most recent eligible trade. It depends on the venue, session, order instructions, available depth and timing. The same security can have a best bid of ₹99.90, a best ask of ₹100.10 and a last trade of ₹100.00 at the same moment. None of those numbers means the same thing.

{{i:lightbulb}} Intuition

Think of price discovery as a sequence of agreements, not a survey that asks everyone what the asset is worth. A thin agreement can move the displayed price without making the whole company worth more in cash.

Markets also constrain trading

Trading sessions, price bands, circuit breakers and surveillance rules are part of the market mechanism. They can slow or pause activity when prices move sharply, but they cannot guarantee an orderly exit. A lower price band with no buyers is not liquidity.
