Market Data

A market-data field is a definition tied to a venue, session, adjustment method and timestamp.

OHLCV compresses a period

Open₹102
High₹108
Low₹99
Close₹105
Volume8.4 lakh

{{tag:hyp}} The open and close depend on the venue's session rules. High and low describe executed prices, not every quote. Volume counts trading activity under a defined convention; it does not say whether buyers or sellers “won”, because every executed trade has both.

Different grains answer different questions

Daily bars

Compact time-series analysis, but hide the path and order sequence inside the day.

Tick data

Event-level records such as trades or quote updates, with much higher storage and cleaning demands.

Order-book data

Displayed prices and quantities at several levels, useful for liquidity and execution analysis.

Corporate-action data

Events needed to reconcile units, cash distributions and historical series.

Raw price, adjusted price and total return

A split can make an unadjusted chart appear to crash even when holder value is unchanged. An adjusted-price series modifies earlier observations so returns can be compared across mechanical unit changes. A total-return series also incorporates cash distributions under stated reinvestment assumptions.

{{i:calculator}} Worked example

{{tag:hyp}}

A share starts at ₹100, ends at ₹106 and pays a ₹4 dividend. Price return is 6%. Total return is (106 + 4) ÷ 100 - 1 = 10%, before tax and reinvestment timing differences.

Data questions before analysis

  1. What exactly does each field mean?
  2. Which venue, security identifier, currency and time zone apply?
  3. Is the series raw, split-adjusted, dividend-adjusted or total return?
  4. Are timestamps event time or receipt time?
  5. Are missing values, suspended sessions and corrections represented?
